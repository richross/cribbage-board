## What the crib is

After the deal, before anyone plays a card, every player discards from their hand into a separate face-down pile called the **crib**. Once everyone has discarded, the crib always ends up with exactly four cards — same as a normal hand — and at the end of the round it's counted for points **just like a hand, except it belongs entirely to the dealer.**

That's the trade-off built into cribbage: the dealer gets first look at an extra hand's worth of points, and everyone else gets to peg first and count first. The advantage evens out over the course of a game as the deal rotates.

## How many cards each player throws

| Players | Each player discards | Crib ends up with |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2 cards | 4 cards (2 from each player) |
| 3 | 1 card | 4 cards (1 from each of 3 players, plus 1 dealt face down at the start) |
| 4 (2 vs 2 partners) | 1 card | 4 cards (1 from each of the 4 players) |

Discards go in face down and stay hidden until the show, no matter whose crib it is.

## Whose crib is it, this hand?

The crib always belongs to whoever is dealing that hand. When it's your crib, you want to keep it strong; when it's your opponent's crib, you want to keep it weak, because any points sitting in there go to them at the end.

## A word on strategy

You don't have to overthink your discards, but a couple of habits go a long way:

- **Into your own crib:** throw cards that work well together — a 5 with anything worth 10 (making an easy fifteen later), or two cards that are close in rank (a potential pair or run).
- **Into your opponent's crib:** throw your most disconnected, awkward cards — things far apart in rank and in different suits — so they're less likely to combine into anything once the starter is added.
- **Watch out for 5s** either way. A lone 5 pairs beautifully with any 10-value card (10, Jack, Queen, King) to make a fifteen, so a stray 5 is a gift to whoever owns that crib.

None of this is required knowledge to play — it just helps once you're comfortable with the basics.
