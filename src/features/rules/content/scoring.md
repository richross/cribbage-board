## The five ways to score

Every hand (and the crib) is scored the same way: lay out the four cards plus the shared starter, five cards total, and look for these combinations. A single card can count toward more than one of them at the same time.

| Combination | How it scores |
|---|---|
| Fifteen | 2 points for **each distinct group** of cards (two or more) that add up to exactly 15 |
| Pair | 2 points for two cards of the same rank |
| Pair royal | 6 points for three cards of the same rank |
| Double pair royal | 12 points for four cards of the same rank |
| Run | 1 point per card, for the **longest** run of 3 or more consecutive ranks — don't also score the shorter runs inside it |
| Flush (in a hand) | 4 points if all 4 hand cards share a suit, +1 more if the starter matches too (5 total) |
| Flush (in the crib) | 5 points, but only if all 4 crib cards **and** the starter share a suit — four matching without the starter scores nothing |
| His nobs | 1 point for holding the Jack of the same suit as the starter |

A few ground rules tie all of this together: the **ace always counts as 1**, never as a high card, so there's no wraparound run like Queen-King-Ace. Face cards (Jack, Queen, King) are worth **10** when you're adding up fifteens, but they keep their own rank — Jack, then Queen, then King — when you're checking for runs.

## Fifteens: count every combination

Fifteens are the most generous part of scoring because *every* subset of two or more cards that sums to 15 counts separately, even if they share cards with each other.

> **Example:** A hand of 5♥, 6♣, 9♦, with the starter 4♠. Two different groups add to 15: {6♣, 9♦} (6+9) and {5♥, 6♣, 4♠} (5+6+4). That's two separate fifteens, worth **2 + 2 = 4 points.**

## Runs: longest only, but duplicates multiply

Score only the single longest run available — don't also count the shorter run tucked inside it. But if a rank in that run is duplicated (a pair sitting inside the sequence), you score the run once for each way it can be formed, plus the pair itself.

| Pattern in the 5 cards | What it's called | Points |
|---|---|---|
| One run of 3, no duplicates | Run of 3 | 3 |
| One run of 4, no duplicates | Run of 4 | 4 |
| One run of 5 | Run of 5 | 5 |
| A run of 3 with one rank doubled (e.g. 3,4,4,5) | Double run of 3 | 8 (two runs of 3 = 6, plus the pair = 2) |
| A run of 3 with one rank tripled (e.g. 3,4,4,4,5) | Triple run | 15 (three runs of 3 = 9, plus the pair royal = 6) |
| A run of 3 with two different ranks doubled (e.g. 3,3,4,4,5) | Double-double run | 16 (four runs of 3 = 12, plus two pairs = 4) |
| A run of 4 with one rank doubled (e.g. 3,4,5,5,6) | Double run of 4 | 10 (two runs of 4 = 8, plus the pair = 2) |

## Flushes: hand vs. crib is different

A flush in your own hand only needs your 4 hand cards to match — the starter is a bonus point if it happens to match too. A flush sitting in the crib is stricter: it needs **all five cards**, hand cards plus starter, to share a suit. Four matching crib cards with a mismatched starter score nothing at all for the flush.

## His nobs

If you're holding the Jack that shares its suit with the starter, that's **1 point**, no matter what else is in your hand. Don't confuse this with his heels (covered in the Starter section) — nobs is about a Jack in a *hand or the crib*, while heels is about the starter itself being a Jack.

## Worked hand: the maximum, 29

The highest score any single hand can ever produce is **29 points**, and it only happens one way: your hand holds three 5s and a Jack, the starter is the fourth 5, and that Jack shares its suit with the starter.

> **Example:** Hand = 5♠, 5♣, 5♦, J♥. Starter = 5♥.
>
> - **Fifteens:** any three of the four 5s make 15 (5+5+5), and there are four different ways to choose three 5s out of four — that's 4 fifteens (4 × 2 = 8 points). Separately, any one 5 plus the Jack also makes 15 (5+10), and there are four 5s to pair with the Jack — that's 4 more fifteens (4 × 2 = 8 points). Fifteens subtotal: **16 points.**
> - **Pairs:** four 5s means every pair of them counts — that's a double pair royal, worth **12 points.**
> - **His nobs:** the Jack♥ matches the starter's suit (hearts) — **1 point.**
> - **Total: 16 + 12 + 1 = 29 points.**

## Why "19" means zero

There's an old cribbage joke about "a 19 hand" — because it's mathematically impossible to score exactly 19 points from a single hand, "I got a 19" is table slang for scoring nothing at all. If someone at your table says it, they mean zero, said with a smile.

## Worked hand: a double run

> **Example:** Hand = 4♠, 5♥, 5♦, 6♣. Starter = 2♦.
>
> - **Fifteens:** 4+5+6 makes 15, and it works twice — once using the 5♥ and once using the 5♦ — for 2 fifteens (**4 points**). The 2♦ doesn't combine with anything else to make another 15.
> - **Runs:** sorted, the relevant cards are 4-5-5-6, which forms two overlapping runs of 3 (4-5-6, using either 5). That's a double run of 3: two runs of three cards (3+3=6) plus the pair of 5s (2) = **8 points.**
> - **Total: 4 + 8 = 12 points.**

## Worked hand: a flush in hand vs. crib

> **Example (hand flush):** Hand = 2♣, 6♣, 9♣, K♣ — all clubs. Starter = 4♦ (a diamond, doesn't match).
>
> - **Fifteens:** 6+9=15 (2 points), and 2+9+4=15 using the starter (2 points). Fifteens subtotal: **4 points.**
> - **Pairs/runs:** none — every rank is different and none are consecutive.
> - **Flush:** all 4 hand cards are clubs, but the starter is a diamond, so it's a 4-card flush only — **4 points** (no +1, since the starter doesn't match).
> - **Total: 4 + 4 = 8 points.**

> **Example (crib flush):** Crib = 3♦, 7♦, 8♦, K♦ — all diamonds. Starter = 2♦ — also a diamond.
>
> - **Fifteens:** 7+8=15 (2 points), and 3+K+2 (3+10+2)=15 (2 points). Fifteens subtotal: **4 points.**
> - **Flush:** all four crib cards *and* the starter are diamonds — every card matches, so the crib scores the full **5 points.** (Had the starter been any other suit, this flush would have scored nothing at all — a crib flush needs all five.)
> - **Total: 4 + 5 = 9 points.**

## Worked hand: zero, a genuine "19"

> **Example:** Hand = 8♠, 9♥, K♣, Q♦. Starter = 3♠.
>
> - **Fifteens:** checking every combination of these five cards (8, 9, 10, 10, 3) — none of them add up to 15.
> - **Pairs:** no two cards share a rank (King and Queen are both worth 10 for fifteens, but they're different ranks, so they don't pair).
> - **Runs:** 8-9 are consecutive and Queen-King are consecutive, but neither reaches three in a row.
> - **Flush:** four different suits in hand — no flush.
> - **Nobs:** no Jack in the hand at all.
> - **Total: 0 points.** A genuine "19."
