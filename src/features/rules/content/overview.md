## What is cribbage?

Cribbage is a card game for two people (or three, or two teams of two) built around a running card count and a hand of cards you count for points afterward. It's played with a standard 52-card deck and, unlike most card games, it's scored on a physical board with pegs instead of a running tally on paper. Watching your opponent's pegs creep up the board is half the fun.

The goal is simple: **be the first player (or team) to score 121 points.** Points come from two places every hand — a bit of quick scoring during the card-by-card play (called **pegging**), and a bigger chunk of scoring afterward when everyone counts their hand (called **the show**).

## The board and the peg race

A cribbage board has two rows of 60 holes per player (121 wins because you also score the very last point to "go out"), plus a few extra game holes. Each player uses two pegs and moves them in a leapfrog pattern: peg your new score, and the peg that's now in the back becomes the marker for your next move forward. That leapfrogging makes it easy to see, at a glance, both your current score and how many points you just added — the back peg shows the "before" and the front peg shows the "after."

No board handy? Any running score works the same way — the board is a scorekeeping tool, not a rule.

## How a hand flows

Every hand of cribbage follows the same five beats:

1. **Deal** — cards are dealt to each player.
2. **Discard to the crib** — everyone tucks a card or two away into a separate hand called the crib, which belongs to the dealer.
3. **Cut** — a card is cut and turned face up. This is the **starter**, and it's shared by everyone later.
4. **Pegging** — players lay cards down one at a time, calling out the running total, scoring small points along the way.
5. **The show** — everyone counts their hand (four cards plus the starter) for points, and finally the dealer counts the crib too.

Then the deal passes to the next player and you do it all again, hand after hand, until someone reaches 121.

## Who's playing?

Cribbage is most commonly played **one-on-one**, and that's the version most people learn first. It also works nicely with **three players** (each with their own hand, but the crib gets a little help from an extra dealt card) or as **two teams of two** sitting across from their partners. The core rhythm — deal, discard, cut, peg, show — stays the same in every version; only the number of cards and a few housekeeping details change. Those variants are covered later in their own sections.
