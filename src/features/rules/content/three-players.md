## The core idea

Three-player cribbage keeps every rule from the two-player game intact — same board, same race to 121, same scoring — but adjusts the deal and the crib to make three hands divide evenly into one crib of four cards.

## Deal

Each of the three players is dealt **5 cards**. Then, before anyone looks at discarding, **one more card is dealt face down straight into the crib** — so the crib starts the hand already holding a card nobody has seen or chosen.

## Discarding

Each player then discards **1 card** from their hand of 5 into the crib. With three single-card discards plus the one dealt face down at the start, the crib ends up with its usual 4 cards, same as always. Everyone keeps 4 cards in hand for pegging and the show.

## The cut

The player sitting to the dealer's left cuts the deck for the starter, exactly as in the two-player game.

## Pegging

Play proceeds clockwise starting with the player to the dealer's left. With three players in the mix, it's common for one or even two players to run out of playable cards (say "go") while the third still has cards left to lay — pegging simply continues around the table, skipping anyone who's already said "go" or played their last card, until nobody has a card that can be legally played. At that point the count resets to 0 as usual, and whoever played the final card before the reset scores for the go (or the 31).

## The show

Counting still follows the same rotation as the deal: starting with the player to the dealer's left, going clockwise, with the dealer counting their own hand last and the crib after that.

## Deal rotation

After each hand, the deal passes one seat to the left, same as ever — it just takes three hands instead of two to cycle all the way back to the same dealer.
