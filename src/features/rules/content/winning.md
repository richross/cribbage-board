## First to 121 wins — instantly

Cribbage is a race to **121 points**, and the moment either player reaches it, the game ends immediately. It doesn't matter if it happens in the middle of pegging or partway through someone else's turn to count in the show — the instant a player's peg crosses 121, the game is over right then. There's no need to finish out the hand or let the other player catch up.

This is also exactly why counting order in the show matters so much (see the Show section): if you're close to 121, counting first can mean winning before your opponent ever gets a chance to count a bigger hand of their own.

## Skunks

Some tables like to raise the stakes a little with an informal bonus called a **skunk**. If the losing player hasn't managed to pass 90 points by the time the winner hits 121 — meaning they finish the game at 90 or below — the winner is said to have "skunked" them. A skunk is commonly worth **double**, as if the winner had won two games instead of one.

## Double skunks

If the losing player is even further behind — stuck at 60 points or below when the game ends — that's a **double skunk**, worth even more by whatever multiplier your table has agreed on.

## These are house rules

Skunks and double skunks aren't part of the core rules of reaching 121 — they're a widely used but informal way of making a lopsided game sting (or celebrate) a bit more, especially when playing for something. Point values and thresholds can vary a little table to table, so it's worth a quick "do we play skunks?" before the game if it matters to you.
