## Setting up

Partnership cribbage seats four players around the table with **partners sitting directly across from each other**, so play alternates between the two teams as it moves around: you, opponent, your partner, other opponent, and back to you.

## Deal and discard

Each player is dealt **5 cards** and discards **1 card** to the crib, exactly like the three-player game, so the crib ends up with its usual 4 cards — one discard from each of the four players. Everyone keeps 4 cards for pegging and the show.

## One crib, one team score

The crib belongs to whichever player is dealing that hand — but since that dealer's points go toward their whole partnership, the crib effectively belongs to the **dealer's team** for that hand. There's only one peg (or one pair of pegs) per team on the board: partners' points combine onto a single shared team track, rather than each player keeping their own separate score.

## Pegging

Play still moves clockwise around the table, one card at a time, alternating between the two teams as it passes from player to player. All the usual pegging rules apply — fifteens, pairs, runs, go, and 31 — and whoever is currently facing you might be your partner or your opponent depending on the round, but the count and the rules don't care which.

## The show

Counting follows the seating order clockwise starting from the player to the dealer's left, same as always, with the dealer's hand counted last, followed by the crib.

## No coaching

Partners play their own hands and discards independently — you don't get to look at your partner's cards or tell them what to keep, discard, or play. Table talk about the game in progress (hinting at your hand, suggesting a discard, or signaling a plan) isn't allowed between partners; you find out how your partner played the same way your opponents do, by watching the table.
