## Terms

**Crib** — A separate four-card hand built from every player's discards. It belongs to the dealer and is counted last, after everyone's own hands.

**Cut** — The action of lifting part of the deck to reveal a card, either to decide who deals first or to reveal the starter before pegging.

**Deal** — The act of distributing cards to each player at the start of a hand; also used to mean the dealer's turn or "whose deal it is."

**Dealer** — The player who deals the cards for a given hand, receives the crib, and counts their own hand and the crib last in the show.

**Double run** — A run of three consecutive ranks where one of those ranks is duplicated (a pair sitting inside the sequence), worth 8 points total: two runs of three (6) plus the pair (2).

**Double skunk** — An informal, bigger version of a skunk: the losing player finishes at 60 points or below when the winner reaches 121.

**Fifteen** — Any group of two or more cards that adds up to exactly 15, worth 2 points, whether found during the show or made during pegging.

**Flush** — Four (or, in some cases, five) cards of the same suit. In a hand, 4 matching cards score 4 points, plus 1 more if the starter also matches. In the crib, only a full 5-card match counts, worth 5 points.

**Go** — The call made during pegging when a player can't play a card without pushing the running count past 31. The last card played before the count resets scores 1 point (2 instead, if that card made exactly 31).

**His heels** — The 2 points the dealer scores immediately if the starter (the cut card) turns out to be a Jack.

**His nobs** — The 1 point scored for holding the Jack that shares its suit with the starter, in your hand or the crib.

**Hole** — One of the individual pegging positions on a cribbage board; moving your peg forward is often described as advancing it "hole by hole."

**Lurch** — An older, less common word for a skunk: winning the game while the loser hasn't managed to pass 90 points.

**Muggins** — An optional rule letting an opponent claim any points you missed while counting your own hand out loud. Off by default at most casual tables.

**Nineteen** — Table slang for a score of zero, since it's mathematically impossible to actually score 19 points from a single hand.

**Pair royal** — Three cards of the same rank played consecutively during pegging, or held together in a hand or crib, worth 6 points.

**Double pair royal** — Four cards of the same rank, worth 12 points.

**Peg** — Either the physical marker moved along the cribbage board to track score, or the verb for scoring points ("to peg 2 for a fifteen").

**Pegging** — The card-by-card phase of play where players lay down cards one at a time, building a running count and scoring fifteens, pairs, runs, and the go as they happen.

**Run** — Three or more cards forming consecutive ranks (such as 4, 5, 6), regardless of the order they were played or held, worth 1 point per card.

**Show** — The counting phase after pegging, where each hand and finally the crib are scored for fifteens, pairs, runs, flushes, and nobs.

**Skunk** — An informal bonus, commonly worth double, awarded when the losing player fails to pass 90 points by the time the winner reaches 121.

**Starter** — The card cut and turned face up after discarding, shared by every hand and the crib during the show (but not used during pegging).

**Street** — An older, informal name sometimes used for a run of consecutive cards.

**Thirty-one** — The maximum the running count can reach during pegging. Hitting exactly 31 scores 2 points.

**Twenty-nine hand** — The highest possible score from a single hand: 29 points, made from three 5s and a Jack in hand with the fourth 5 as the starter, where the Jack matches the starter's suit.
