## What the show is

Once every card has been played out in pegging, it's time to count. Each player gathers back up the same four cards they played during pegging, lays them out together with the shared starter — five cards in total — and counts up every scoring combination they can find: fifteens, pairs, runs, flushes, and his nobs. All of that scoring is explained card-by-card in the Scoring section — this section is about the order everyone counts in and why that order matters.

## Counting order

Counting always goes in the same rotation as the deal:

1. **The player to the dealer's left counts first**, out loud, so everyone can follow and check the math.
2. Play continues clockwise around the table, each player counting their hand in turn.
3. **The dealer counts their own hand last.**
4. **Finally, the dealer counts the crib** — the extra hand built from everyone's discards, which belongs to them alone.

So in a two-player game, it's simply: non-dealer's hand, then dealer's hand, then the crib.

## Why the order matters

Cribbage games are usually won mid-count, not at the start of a fresh hand. Because the first player to reach 121 wins **immediately** — even in the middle of pegging, even in the middle of the show — going first in the counting order is a real advantage near the end of a close game. If the non-dealer is sitting one good hand away from 121, they get to count first and potentially end the game before the dealer, whose hand and crib might have scored even more, ever gets a chance to count at all.

This is also why the dealer's crib is counted dead last: it's the dealer's built-in consolation for pegging second and counting last every single hand.

## Keeping each other honest

Because everyone counts out loud and the cards stay on the table for all to see, mistakes get caught in the moment. If a player skips points they were entitled to, there's an optional house rule for that — see the Muggins section for how that works and why many casual tables leave it switched off.
