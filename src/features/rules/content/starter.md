## Cutting the starter

Once every player has discarded to the crib, it's time to reveal one more card that everyone will share. The player to the dealer's left cuts the deck, and the dealer turns face up the top card of the lower portion — the part that was lifted off. That card is called the **starter** (sometimes just "the cut").

The starter sits face up on top of the deck (or on top of the crib, depending on table habit) for the rest of the hand, in plain view of everyone.

## His heels: a Jack pays out immediately

If the starter turns out to be a **Jack**, the dealer scores **2 points immediately**, before pegging even begins. This bonus is traditionally called **his heels**, and it's the dealer's little reward for having to wait until last to count their own hand and crib.

> **Example:** The starter is cut and it's the Jack♦. The dealer pegs 2 points on the spot, right then — no discussion needed, no waiting for the show.

## What the starter is used for

The starter does two different jobs during a hand:

- **In the show**, the starter joins every hand and the crib as a fifth card. Each player's four cards plus the starter are counted together for fifteens, pairs, runs, flushes, and nobs.
- **In pegging**, the starter does *not* come into play at all. It's not laid down, it's not part of the running count, and nobody "plays" it. It simply waits on the side until it's needed for counting.

Because everyone shares the same starter, it's often the card that turns an ordinary hand into something special — or the reason a promising hand falls a little flat. Either way, it's the same one card for every hand and the crib alike.
