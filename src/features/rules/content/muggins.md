## What muggins is

When you count your hand out loud during the show, it's easy to miss a fifteen buried in the middle of your cards, or forget a run when you're focused on pairs. **Muggins** is an optional rule that lets an alert opponent call out "muggins!" the moment they spot a missed combination, and **claim those uncounted points for themselves** instead of letting them go to waste.

> **Example:** You count your hand and announce "fifteen two, and a pair is four" — but you missed a second fifteen hiding in there. If muggins is in play, your opponent can speak up, point out the fifteen you skipped, and peg those 2 points onto their own score instead of yours.

## Why tables use it

Muggins rewards paying close attention to every hand, not just your own, and adds a friendly bit of tension to the counting phase — nobody wants to hand free points to the other side by rushing through their count.

## Why many tables leave it off

For casual, friendly games, muggins can feel like it's punishing a simple arithmetic slip rather than rewarding good play, so plenty of tables skip it entirely and just let players correct their own counting mistakes without penalty. **It's off by default** unless your table specifically agrees to use it — worth a quick check before the game starts if you want the sharper-edged version.
